Create a third navigation exploration as a separate version of the existing Aspen Snowmass prototype.

This exploration should investigate a more immersive, progressive navigation model inspired by the cascading flyout behavior used by Eleven Experiences.

The goal is NOT to replicate Eleven’s visual design. Use it only as an interaction reference: selections progressively reveal additional levels of information, allowing users to move from a broad category toward a more informed decision without exposing the entire information architecture at once.

Core hypothesis

Can the navigation progressively help users understand and choose between Aspen Snowmass experiences before requiring them to leave the menu?

For Aspen Snowmass, explore a shallower but richer cascading hierarchy.

A possible progression could be:

* Primary navigation category
* Relevant options within that category
* Richer contextual information for the selected option

For example, selecting Four Mountains could progressively reveal Aspen Mountain, Aspen Highlands, Buttermilk, and Snowmass. Selecting a mountain could then reveal richer context such as imagery, a short personality/description, relevant information, and a clear primary CTA.

Do not feel constrained to this exact layout or number of panels. Determine the strongest interaction and spatial composition for the cascading model.

Progressive discovery

Keep the default/closed navigation relatively simple.

The richer experience should emerge progressively through interaction rather than displaying the entire information architecture immediately.

Avoid creating an unnecessarily deep hierarchy. Progressive disclosure should make decision-making easier, not introduce additional navigation work.

Persistent high-value utilities

High-value mountain utilities must remain persistently accessible within the expanded navigation.

Trail Maps, Weather, Mountain Cameras, Lift Status, Parking, and other established utility links should NOT become buried inside the cascading hierarchy.

Explore how these can live as a consistent utility layer across the expanded navigation, similar to the persistent utility treatment explored in previous concepts.

The cascading experience above can change based on the user’s selection, while this utility layer remains predictable and immediately accessible for high-intent users who may come to the site specifically for this information.

Seasonality

Preserve the Winter / Summer toggle as an important part of the navigation.

Consider how switching seasons can dynamically change relevant content throughout the cascading experience, including navigation options, activities, contextual information, and imagery.

The season selection should influence the experience rather than functioning only as a visual toggle.

Four Mountains

Continue exploring the four mountains as distinct experiences rather than simply four links.

Use the short personality descriptions as a way to help users quickly understand the differences between Aspen Mountain, Aspen Highlands, Buttermilk, and Snowmass.

The cascading model should provide an opportunity to introduce useful context without requiring users to visit each mountain page first.

Visual direction

This exploration can be more visual and immersive than the previous navigation concepts.

Use Aspen Snowmass imagery intentionally as part of the progressive experience. Consider how imagery, scale, layering, transitions, and changing content can reinforce the user’s selection.

Maintain the existing Aspen Snowmass visual language, typography, interaction patterns, CTAs, and brand character wherever appropriate.

This is an exploration of navigation architecture, interaction, and layout, not a brand redesign.

Do not change established CTA styles, link behaviors, or other existing interaction patterns unnecessarily.

Global actions

Keep Book Now clearly and consistently accessible.

Maintain access to the existing global actions and controls where appropriate rather than hiding them inside the cascading hierarchy.

Prototype behavior

Build this as a working interaction, not simply a static visual composition.

We should be able to evaluate:

* Opening and closing the navigation
* Moving through the cascading hierarchy
* Selecting different mountains/options
* How contextual information changes with selection
* Winter / Summer switching
* Persistent access to the high-value utility links

Transitions between states should help communicate hierarchy and progression without becoming decorative or slowing down navigation.

Exploration goal

The final result should feel meaningfully different from the previous Original Navigation Evolved, consolidated mega-menu, and Classic Horizontal explorations.

Those concepts primarily explored reorganizing and exposing information within a mega-menu structure.

This version should test a different idea:

A simplified entry point that progressively becomes richer and more immersive as the user makes decisions, while maintaining immediate access to the practical mountain information frequent and high-intent visitors need.

Push the spatial composition and progressive interaction model enough for us to properly evaluate this direction, while keeping the experience usable and grounded in Aspen Snowmass’s actual navigation needs.
